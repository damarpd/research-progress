## Progress SLR

### Developing the SLR from scratch

- Guideline: *Systematic Literature Review Guide: Boarding & Alighting at BRT Stations* (Claude artifact), which uses PRISMA 2020 for reporting and Wohlin (2014) for snowballing.
- Decision: run a new SLR rather than an "update", because the earlier review draft was a narrative review, not a systematic one.
- Main risk: selection bias. Articles already known tend to be the ones that shaped my thinking, so including them automatically would make the review non-reproducible.
- **Guiding principle**: prior articles test and feed the systematic search; they never bypass it.
- Five roles of prior articles:
    1. Inventory: audit trail before the SLR starts
    2. Benchmark (validation) set: test whether the search string retrieves relevant known papers
    3. "Other methods" source: prior articles missed by the database search enter through the other-methods column of PRISMA 2020
    4. Same criteria: screened and quality-appraised exactly like new records
    5. Snowball seeds: included prior articles start backward and forward citation searching

### Step 1: PICOC and research questions

- Scope: multi-berth BRT stations; shared versus direction-separated platform layouts; surveillance video within a fundamental diagram framework; passenger movement model for design and policy.
- Two tiers: **core** (BRT stations) and **transferable** (metro, rail, bus platform-vehicle interfaces, only if boarding/alighting at the platform is examined). Reason: BRT-specific literature is thin, and none of the 15 prior articles is a core BRT study.

| element | definition |
|---|---|
| Population | passengers boarding and alighting at public transport platforms; multi-berth BRT stations are the target |
| Intervention / exposure | platform design and layout: shared versus direction-separated flows, berth configuration, door and platform geometry |
| Comparison | layout alternatives, single-berth versus multi-berth, crowd levels, other modes in the transferable tier |
| Outcomes | crowd dynamics (flow, density, speed, fundamental diagram), boarding/alighting time, dwell time, conflicts |
| Context | BRT stations (core) and metro/bus platform-vehicle interfaces (transferable); video-based observation, experiments and simulation |

- RQ1 (layout): how does platform layout affect boarding and alighting performance at BRT stations and comparable platforms?
- RQ2 (crowd dynamics): which crowd-dynamic characteristics have been established, especially through fundamental diagrams, and how were they measured from surveillance video or other observation?
- RQ3 (modeling): which passenger movement models have been used for boarding/alighting and platform design, and how were they calibrated and validated?
- RQ4 (gap): what gaps remain for multi-berth BRT platforms? Answered by synthesis of RQ1-RQ3, not by a separate search.
- Decision: the link between boarding/alighting time and bus bunching stays in the introduction as background, so a fifth RQ on bunching was dropped. It would pull the search toward operational studies away from platform level.

### Notes on building search keywords

- PRISMA 2020 does not prescribe how to build keywords. It requires reporting the full search strategy for every database (items 6 and 7); PRISMA-S extends this for searches.
- Workflow:
    1. Break the RQs into search concepts (2-4 blocks). Outcomes and comparison are usually not searched, since extra AND blocks reduce recall.
    2. Collect terms for each concept: words from known papers (title, abstract, author keywords), spelling variants, abbreviations, broader and narrower terms, pilot results.
    3. Combine: OR within a concept, AND between concepts, NOT rarely; quotes for phrases, `*` for truncation, proximity operators (`W/n` in Scopus, `NEAR/n` in Web of Science).
    4. Pilot, test recall on the hidden validation half, find out why each paper was missed, revise, and log every version.
- Scopus: `TITLE-ABS-KEY(...)` with year and language limits; Web of Science: `TS=(...)` with filters. Translate and test each database separately.

## New terms

- **SLR** (systematic literature review): a review with a written protocol, reproducible search, and explicit screening and appraisal
- **PRISMA 2020**: reporting guideline and flow diagram for systematic reviews, with a database column and an "other methods" column
- **PICOC**: Population, Intervention/exposure, Comparison, Outcomes, Context; used to structure the research questions
- **Benchmark (validation) set**: known relevant articles used to measure the recall of the search string
- **Snowballing**: backward (reference lists) and forward (citing papers) searching from included papers
- **Recall (search sensitivity)**: share of the benchmark articles that the search string retrieves
- **Precision**: share of retrieved records that are relevant; low values (5-20%) are normal for systematic searches
- **Two-tier scope**: core (BRT) and transferable (metro/bus) studies, analyzed separately
- **Quality appraisal**: judging how trustworthy a relevant study's evidence is, separate from judging relevance
- **Boolean operators**: OR joins synonyms, AND joins concepts, NOT excludes terms
- **Truncation**: a symbol such as `*` that matches word forms (`board*` finds boarding, boarded)
- **Deduplication**: removing the same paper found more than once (across databases or within one), so that each paper is screened once
- **Cohen's kappa**: agreement between two reviewers beyond chance, reported for the second reviewer's sample
- **OSF Registries**: Open Science Framework platform for time-stamped, read-only protocol registration
